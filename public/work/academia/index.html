Why the Lyceum

Small by design, not by accident.

Twelve in a room

Seminars cap at twelve. Everyone speaks, everyone is read, and nobody hides at the back.

Primary texts first

You read the book, the letter, the judgment. Commentaries come after you have formed a view.

One tutor, every week

Each student meets a tutor for a written tutorial. It is the oldest part of the college and the part alumni miss most.

A house, not a campus

Two residence halls, a dining room and a library in one square. You will know the cooks by name.

The faculty

People who read for a living.

Twenty-two full-time faculty, no adjuncts. Every tutor teaches first-year seminars, whatever their seniority.

Campus & community

A quiet place to think out loud.

The college sits on eleven acres above the river: a library, two houses, a dining room and a walled garden. There is no stadium, no fraternity row and no lecture theatre with a screen bigger than the blackboard.

What there is: a debating society that meets on Thursdays, a student journal, a choir, and a kitchen that serves dinner at seven sharp. Most students live on site for all three years.

  • All rooms single, all bills included
  • Dinner with faculty three nights a week
  • Library open until midnight, every night
  • Sixty minutes by train to the city

Questions

Before you write to us.

Do I need to know what I want to study?

No. The first year is shared across all three disciplines, and you choose a major at the end of it. About a third of students change their mind after the first term.

Is there an entrance exam?

No. We ask for a written sample and a short letter, then a conversation. We are looking for how you think, not how well you have been coached.

Can I study part time?

The college is full time and residential, because the seminars and the common room are the course. We do run a two-week summer school for people who want a taste of it first.

What support is available for disabled students?

Every building has step-free access, the library has a dedicated quiet room, and the college funds note-taking and assistive software where needed. Talk to us early and we will plan it with you.

Is the college religious?

No. The Lyceum was founded by a reading society and has no religious affiliation. Students and staff come from many traditions, and none.
