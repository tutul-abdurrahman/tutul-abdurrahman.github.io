Research

Research interests

Four areas, one habit: reading closely, in two languages, against the grain of the syllabus.

Postcolonial literature

South Asian writing, colonial print, and the politics of citation, who gets annotated, and by whom.

Translation studies

What Bengali does to English, and what translation does to the idea of an original text.

Literary pedagogy

Syllabus design as an argument, and the classroom as a place where close reading is still taught slowly.

Print & periodical history

Little magazines, departmental journals, and the short, stubborn life of literary print in Dhaka.

About

A slow reader, by profession

I have taught English literature in Dhaka for sixteen years, mostly to students who read in two languages and write in three. My research began with a simple irritation: the postcolonial canon arrived already annotated, and the annotations rarely asked what a reader in Dhaka did with the text. Translation studies gave me better questions.

These days I divide my time between a book on citation practices in South Asian literary criticism, an ongoing translation of nineteenth-century Bengali essays, and the unglamorous work of reading drafts closely. I supervise dissertations on postcolonial reading practices, translation ethics, and literary pedagogy.

I am available for PhD supervision inquiries, translation workshops, and departmental seminars. Email is the reliable way to reach me.
