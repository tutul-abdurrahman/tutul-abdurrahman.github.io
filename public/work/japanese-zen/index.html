A Koan

Empty the cup, and the room grows.

We built Ma because every good thing we know happens in the pause, not the to-do. The studio fires twice a season; the tea room seats eight and no more. Nothing here is a funnel, a queue, or an upsell. Come for an hour, hold a warm bowl, and let the street noise fade to the tick of the kettle. If the silence feels strange for the first ten minutes, stay for twenty, it stops being empty and starts being room.

工房の心 · The heart of the studio
The Craft

Slow clay, single-fire, wood and ash.

Three working principles run through every bowl, every blend, every seating at Ma. They are not slogans, they schedule our year.

Thrown by two hands

Every bowl leaves the wheel of Aya or Senior apprentice Kenji, 90 pieces a season, never more. Blanks are dated under the foot, so each cup tells you which Thursday it was born.

Whisked in front of you

No tea arrives hidden in paper. Usucha is whisked at the low table and handed over while the foam still breathes. Thick koicha is shared from one bowl, as the old rule asks.

Fired once, in wood

Our anagama kiln burns split red pine for 52 hours each March and September. Ash settles where it likes, no two glaze runs have ever matched, and we have stopped apologising for it.

安らい · What settles
What Settles

Six things guests say they took home.

Slower mornings

Guests keep the whisk timer card, 2 minutes of quiet before the first screen. 6 out of 10 tell us they still use it a year on.

Hands that remember

Wheel practice rewires patience. Your third bowl spins truer than your first, and the difference was never talent, it was breath.

A table set for few

Eight chairs. One schedule. No standing groups, no party noise, conversation arrives at the size a teapot serves.

Tea that tastes of place

Single-garden matcha from Uji, steamed in 20 kg lots. May and autumn picks sit side by side on the shelf, labelled by field, not by brand rank.

Gifts that need no wrapping

A kiln-fired bowl travels in a plain paulownia box with the maker's seal stamped on the lid, paper, ink, done.

Wabi as permission

The uneven brim, the nicked glaze, the shadowed cup, the flaw is not tolerated here, it is requested.

問い · Questions
Six Questions

Asked quietly, answered plainly.

None. The host walks each first-timer through the whisk, the bow, and the turn of the bowl in the first five minutes. The form is simple on purpose, the point is the sitting, not the exam.

Minutes 0–10: choose your bowl from the open shelves. Minutes 10–40: the pour, matched to the day's weather, with a short telling of where this pick grew. Minutes 40–60: quiet refills of hojicha, studio talk if you want it, silence if you do not.

Yes. Bowls from the last two firings hang by the entry, each tagged with its firing date, from $90 to $340. The shelf of loose teas changes with the May and autumn picks, current stock is 32 cultivars.

Resident members pick a bowl, and we log its foot-mark in the shelf book. It waits, washed and wrapped, between your visits. The oldest shelf bowl has waited 11 years and is ours to guard, yours to claim.

The March and September firings run 52 hours each, unmanned only from 2 to 5 in the morning. Circle members may come for any daylight hour; drop-in guests can book one firing-watch slot per season from the journal page.

Seatings: free to move up to 24 hours before. Memberships: cancel any month by the 20th, no exit fee. That is the whole policy, we would rather spend the ink on tea.
