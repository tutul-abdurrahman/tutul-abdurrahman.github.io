A Declaration

Nine hundred years of prayer, harvest and quiet left these walls with a silence that cannot be built — only kept. When the last monks departed in 1538, the abbey fell to ivy and weather; when our family took it in hand in 2019, every craftsman worked to the old grammar: lime mortar, hand-dressed stone, glass blown in Yorkshire kilns.

We open the abbey for a small number of gatherings each year — weddings in the nave, vigils of music at midsummer, harvest suppers in the cloister — so that each one has the room, and the dark, to itself.

“Stone remembers what the living forget.” — Inscription, north transept

Chapter 2 · The Keeping

Why gatherings here feel different

Versicle and response — what we keep, and what it gives back.

  • One gathering per day, never two

    — the nave stays yours from first light to last candle.

  • Two thousand candles, hand-lit by our sacristans

    — no electric uplighting; the stone is lit as it was meant to be.

  • A steward assigned to your day alone

    — Brother Ambrose's successors still carry the keys, and the calm.

  • Kitchen, vestry and garth included in every hire

    — no hidden cloister fees; the whole abbey answers to one price.

  • Every booking funds the next restoration bay

    — 12% of each hire goes to the fabric fund, stone by stone.
