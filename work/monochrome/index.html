01 / Selected work

Three case studies

Each one is written the way the work happened: what was broken, what I did about it, and what changed afterwards. Outcome figures are sample numbers for this demo.

Case 01

Maya Savings — first-run onboarding

  • Consumer fintech
  • Mobile
  • 2025
Problem
62% of verified users left between OTP confirmation and the first deposit. Support heard the same sentence every week: “I did not know what happens next.”
Approach
Fourteen moderated interviews, a journey map of the first session, then three prototype rounds. I merged two steps, moved the goal amount before the funding screen, and rewrote permissions copy in plain Bangla and English.
Outcome
Fewer exits, faster first deposits, and a design file the engineering team could build from without a meeting.
  • Task success 42% → 89% (sample)
  • Time to deposit 6m 40s → 2m 10s (sample)

Case 02

Trellis Admin — multi-role permissions

  • B2B SaaS
  • Desktop web
  • 2024
Problem
Admins could not tell who could see what. Four support escalations a week, and every new workspace started with a spreadsheet of roles.
Approach
I audited the existing role matrix, modelled object-level permissions in Figma, and added an inline preview that shows a role's visible screens before saving. Handoff included state tables for every permission edge case.
Outcome
Setup became a two-click task, and the pilot ran five weeks with no permission errors reported.
  • Setup time −58% (sample)
  • Permission errors 0 in pilot (sample)

Case 03

Rent ledger — read, print, collect

  • Property management
  • Mobile + print
  • 2024
Problem
Monthly collection ran on paper. Meter readings were written twice — once in the field, once in the ledger — and the tenant copy was often illegible.
Approach
I reversed the flow: read the meter, review the bill, print the slip, then collect and post to the ledger. Six slips per A4, large numerals, and an offline state for basements with no signal.
Outcome
Collection cycles halved, duplicate entries nearly disappeared, and the printed slip became the record both sides trusted.
  • Collection cycle 5 days → 2 days (sample)
  • Re-keyed entries −90% (sample)

02 / Process

Four steps, no theatre

The same sequence every time, scaled to the size of the problem. Research is not a phase I skip when the deadline is close; it is the part that keeps the deadline honest.

  1. 01

    Research

    Interviews, support-ticket reading, and a look at what people actually do instead of what the brief says they do.

    Deliverable — findings note

  2. 02

    Wireframe

    Flows and low-fidelity screens, tested early with three to five users. Cheap to change, honest about gaps.

    Deliverable — flow + grayscale screens

  3. 03

    Visual design

    Type, spacing, and states built on a token set the team can extend without asking me for every screen.

    Deliverable — high-fidelity screens + tokens

  4. 04

    Handoff

    Annotated specs, edge-case tables, and a build review. I stay in the room until the shipped screen matches the file.

    Deliverable — spec + build review

03 / About

A designer who reads the ticket queue

I am a product designer based in Dhaka with five years of experience across fintech, B2B SaaS, and field tools. I came to design through front-end work, which is probably why my files are organised the way engineers wish they were.

Most of my work starts with a support inbox or a research session, not a mood board. I like problems where the interface is only half the answer — the other half is a flow, a sentence of copy, or a decision someone senior has to make.

I work in Figma with a token-based system, write my own handoff notes, and test on a low-end Android phone before I call anything done. Available for product design engagements and design-system work, remote or in Dhaka.
