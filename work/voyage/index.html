STOP 06 · INFORMATION DESK

Frequently asked, plainly answered

Q-01 Do I need to be fit for an expedition?
Every route lists a grade on the departure board — simple as that. Grades 1 and 2 need comfortable walking and a daypack; grade 3 routes (Lapland winter, High Atlas) need stamina for 6–8 hour days. We can move you between grades up to 30 days out at no charge.
Q-02 What is actually included in the fare?
Every in-country leg (rail, ferry, coach, the odd camel), accommodation per your fare class, breakfast as standard, the expedition map, ticket wallet, packing sheet, leader and support guide, and all permits and museum entries on the published route list. Flights to the join point are yours to book.
Q-03 Can I travel alone?
Most of our passengers do — 62% booked solo in 2025. Standard fare means a shared twin room with a matched solo traveler of the same gender; the single supplement is £210 if you prefer your own room.
Q-04 What happens if a leg is disrupted?
This is why crews carry radio and why the announcement panel exists. In 2025, 96% of legs left as scheduled and the rest were re-routed within an average of 2 hours. If a delay runs past a meal stop, the food and the onward ticket are on us.
Q-05 Is there an age limit?
16–85 as a rule of thumb: under-18s travel at half fare with a guardian, and there is no upper limit if your doctor is content with the route grade you have chosen. Our oldest 2025 passenger was 79, on the Andalusia Line.
Q-06 How far out should I book?
Spring and autumn departures fill 4–6 months early; winter Lapland fills by September. The departure boards on this page show exact seat counts per class, and the £60 deposit holds a seat for 48 hours while you check flights.
